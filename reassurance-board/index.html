What is a reassurance board?

Caregivers invented it with a whiteboard and a marker. It answers the questions your loved one asks most, before they have to ask. Here is how to make one today, and when a digital board earns its keep.

The idea, in one minute

When someone lives with cognitive decline, a few questions come back many times a day. What day is it. When is the doctor. Where are you. Who is coming. Each one is really the same question underneath: am I okay, and is everything as it should be.

A reassurance board keeps the answers in plain view, all day, where your loved one already looks. Instead of asking and waiting, they can look and settle. Families across caregiver communities use this trick with a whiteboard on the kitchen wall, and it works because it never sighs, never says "like I said," and never gets tired of answering.

How to make one with a whiteboard

  1. Put it where they already look. The kitchen wall, next to the chair, across from the bed. Not by the front door.
  2. Write today, big. The day of the week largest, then the date. Update it every morning.
  3. Answer their real questions. Not a schedule, an answer. "Sarah will call after lunch." "The doctor is Thursday, and Tom will drive you."
  4. Add one warm line. "You are safe at home. We love you." That line does more than the rest of the board combined.
  5. Take down anything that is no longer true. A stale board teaches them to stop trusting it. Current beats complete.

If you do only this, you have done something real. A $10 whiteboard, updated daily, genuinely helps many families.

Where the whiteboard runs out

  • It only works if someone is there to update it. The day changes at midnight. The board doesn't.
  • It cannot speak. Reading gets harder for many people as cognitive decline progresses. A voice reaches further than a marker.
  • It cannot remind. The 6pm medicine still needs a person to point at the board at 6pm.
  • Distance defeats it. If you live an hour away, or a flight away, the board on her wall is whatever it said the last time someone visited.

The digital reassurance board

A digital reassurance board is the same idea on a screen that keeps itself current. The day and time are always right. The family changes the message from a phone, from anywhere, and it appears on the wall the same second.
